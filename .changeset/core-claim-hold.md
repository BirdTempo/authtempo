Core exports `holdClaim`, `releaseClaim`, and `ClaimLostError` from its root. `holdClaim` takes the first statement of a claim of a user, for a writer that is no claim, such as the importer. On Postgres and MySQL it is an update of the unverified user row, which holds the row lock until the commit. On D1, the SQLite drivers, and the memory store it creates the claim lease. Unlike a claim, it takes no stale lease, because a claim can run past the life of its lease. On a store with no isolation the hold then reads the flag of the user. On a verified user it deletes its own lease and throws `ClaimLostError`, because the sweep can delete a claim's lease before the hold. `holdClaim` returns the expiry of its lease, or null on an isolated store. `releaseClaim` takes that value and deletes the lease by its id and that expiry, in the same transaction. So it never deletes the lease of a claim that took the stale lease of the hold. It answers false when its lease is gone or a claim took it, and the writer then deletes its own rows.

A claim of the user waits for the hold on Postgres and MySQL. On D1 it answers 409 `conflict`. On the SQLite drivers and the memory store it retries after a wait, then answers 409. A lease row of the user, live or stale, throws `ClaimLostError` with `held`. The writer runs no hook between the two calls, because the lease lives 60 seconds. A writer that stops before the release leaves the lease. A claim takes it after 60 seconds. The app's scheduled sweep deletes the lease at its first run 60 seconds or more after its write.
